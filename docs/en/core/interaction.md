# Interaction

_RASER 5.0 particle and laser interactions in a sensor_

Interaction calculates where and when electron–hole pairs are created in a
Device. Particle transport, laser absorption, and a prescribed MIP track all
produce the same two arrays for carrier transport.

## Carrier creation data

`track_position` contains one `[x, y, z, t]` entry for each carrier group.
Positions use detector coordinates in micrometres, and time is expressed in
seconds. `ionized_pairs` contains the corresponding number of electron–hole
pairs. The two arrays have equal length and matching order.

An energy deposit `E_dep` produces

```math
N_{eh}=\frac{E_{\mathrm{dep}}}{W},
```

where `W` is the ionization energy of the Device material. A carrier group may
represent many physical electron–hole pairs through its `ionized_pairs` value.

## Geant4 interaction

The Geant4 calculation uses the geometry, sensitive volumes, material, and
detector-coordinate mapping declared by Device. A source definition supplies
the particle type, energy, initial position, direction, event count, and
Geant4 execution settings. Reusable source definitions are described in
[Source components](../components/source/README.md).

Geant4 transports each primary and records the position and deposited energy
of every step in a sensitive volume. Event totals, step positions, step
energies, and particle directions remain grouped by event. The Device mapping
then converts sensitive-volume positions into detector coordinates.

The deposited energy of each step is divided by the material ionization energy
to produce `ionized_pairs`. The mapped step position and event time produce the
corresponding entry in `track_position`.

## Laser interaction

Laser interaction calculates the carrier density generated by single-photon
absorption (SPA) or two-photon absorption (TPA). Its definition contains the
wavelength, pulse energy, temporal width, spatial width, refractive index,
focus, propagation direction, and spatial integration steps.

For SPA, the carrier density follows the local optical intensity and the
exponential absorption along the beam:

```math
n_{\mathrm{SPA}}(s,r)
=\frac{\alpha\lambda}{h_{\mathrm P}c}
I(s,r)\exp[-\alpha(s+d)].
```

For TPA, the spatial calculation uses the pulse fluence `F(s,r)` and a
unit-area temporal Gaussian `g(t)`. The pulse-integrated carrier density is

```math
n_{\mathrm{TPA}}(s,r)
=\frac{\beta_2\lambda}{2h_{\mathrm P}c}
F^2(s,r)\int g^2(t)\,dt,
```

with

```math
\int g^2(t)\,dt
=\frac{\sqrt{2\ln2}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\tau_{\mathrm{FWHM}}}.
```

Here, `s` is the coordinate along the beam, `r` is the transverse distance
from the beam axis, `d` is the distance from the entry surface to the focus,
and `h_P` is the Planck constant. Top, bottom, and edge injection map these
beam coordinates into detector coordinates.

The spatial mesh integrates the carrier density over each cell. Cell centres
form `track_position`, while the integrated populations form `ionized_pairs`.
The central time and temporal width define the laser pulse profile associated
with these carrier groups.

## Prescribed MIP track

A prescribed MIP track is a straight segment between two detector-coordinate
points. The segment is divided into equal-length packets located at their cell
centres. Its deposition is specified as electron–hole pairs per micrometre or
deposited energy per micrometre.

For a track of length `L` divided into `N` packets, each packet contains

```math
N_{eh}^{\mathrm{packet}}
=\frac{L}{N}\left(\frac{dN_{eh}}{dx}\right).
```

The packet centres and creation time form `track_position`. The packet weights
form `ionized_pairs`.
